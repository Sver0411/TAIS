# v0.1 failure forensics (diagnostic data only)

Baseline: `68af4b6d9df7ed145a237defc4ab920600823373`. No legacy source, configuration, experiment script, report or raw metric is edited. Seeds 20–49 have been exposed and are diagnostic, not independent v0.2 evidence. Commands and machine outputs are under `results/v0.2`; scripts export actual sampled execution rather than full-resolution detector replays.

## Causal chain, checked against code

1. `tais.signals.generate` drives a noise-free trapezoid (4 C or 16 %RH, 5 s ramps), independently labels physical deviations via pinned `label_events`, then draws noise from seed and faults from a separate seed+100000 schedule. Truth starts after the physical threshold crossing, not at a score crossing.
2. Range replaces temperature with 100/200; missing/invalid removes it; stuck replaces it with the pre-event reading. Drift and stable offset are additive. The one-second spike is **60 s before** the event, so its same/cross probes are sequential.
3. The actual SensorTrust C11 context consumes only scheduled samples. Range costs 55 points; SPIKE and DRIFT 25, STUCK 35, missing 55 after three invalid reads. Scores are recomputed from current flags, not calibrated probabilities. A step may be flagged once, then accepted. STUCK requires 20 sampled values within .01; DRIFT needs a 24-sample window and 24 repeated slope verdicts. Neither is a wall-clock timeout.
4. AdaptiveSense deviation is from the previous EMA; STD is 30 s, ROC 10 s. Score>8 for >=10 s debounces an environmental candidate. ALERT entry is a separate >=12 threshold. In v0.1 DEGRADED confirmation is incorrectly easy to *abandon relative to that event criterion*: every new reading must still score>=12. It can cancel a pending episode when score is 8–12 even though the raw event remains active. Persistence is against the previous sampled value, which can already be inside a slowly changing excursion. This is a policy mismatch, not an upstream equation error.
5. FAULT or invalid readings are never scored; pending episodes are discarded. First usable recovery resets the scorer and ladder and seeds current value; C history is retained. This prevents stale-baseline alarms but cannot recover an event that has disappeared.
6. HEALTHY channels retain separate scorers/state/ladders. Shared interval is minimum eligible/confirmation/diagnostic demand, clamped 5–60 s. There is no node-wide health veto. Removing spurious fault urgency can nevertheless lengthen the shared cycle and miss a short healthy event.
7. Upload reasons include first/heartbeat, health **or flag** changes, interval/state changes, healthy deltas, confirmed event onsets. Environmental active episodes are reported at their first upload. `match_events` is channel-aware one-to-one, with onset in [truth start-5,truth end+5]. It measures temporal association, not causal sensitivity.

## Confirmed causes and probes

- **Information erasure:** `v02_identifiability.py` compares event-present and event-absent observation streams for range/missing/invalid/stuck, both profiles, all old 50 seeds. Their canonical observed bytes are identical, while labels differ. No scheduler using that channel can discriminate the pair. `range_same_long` and `missing_same_long` are in this category; increasing confirmation cannot restore the signal.
- **Apparent range recall is not retained event information:** for old test seeds the entire original AdaptiveSense result for range_same equals range_none, profile by profile. Its 18 matched same-channel reports (9 long, 9 short) also occur with no event. This accounts for the **entire 18-event same-channel gap** between AS (127) and v0.1 TAIS (109), not an established loss of 18 observable true events. Reported recall remains unchanged historically; this additional equality check changes interpretation, not historical metrics.
- **GateOnly vs TAIS:** on observable additive same-channel cases AS and TAIS each match 109/120, GateOnly 108/120 in old test; erased cases give AS18/240 and both gates0/240. In development/validation, the same decomposition is exported in `identifiability.json`. This does not establish general recall equivalence outside these observations.
- **Stable offset blind spot:** seed20 `stable_offset_none_long` reports at 610 and1205, while no physical event exists. The 3 C step is below the C spike threshold5 and remains in range. Trust remains healthy at the step; ordinary score/debounce interprets offset and recovery as changes. All three methods produce 57 such false reports across the old 30-seed test. Gating cannot remove a fault the detector considers healthy.
- **Sparse stuck diagnosis:** `stuck_none_long` has no reports and no C STUCK detection. Approximately ten fault-period observations at60 s do not fill the20-sample window with stuck readings. This is a diagnostic blind spot, not proof of a healthy sensor.
- **Sampling/debounce loss:** seed20 `spike_same_short` samples at600 and660 miss the634–656 truth interval; the injected spike572–573 is not sampled. A future-value-aware explanation would be invalid; the exported sample times expose the actual opportunity. Short `range_cross` loses shared fault-induced fast sampling relative to AS; isolation alone cannot guarantee short-event capture.
- **Confirmation churn:** seed20 `drift_same_long` creates candidates975/1040/1105/1170 and cancels at980/1045/1110 (scores9.67/10.33/11.71). The real event634–810 was already reported645. Thus these later confirmation problems are observed **fault-driven false-event opportunities**, not evidence that repairing them will improve primary recall.

## Evidence exports and limits

`events.csv` contains every old-test physical event for AS/GateOnly/TAIS, sampled times, C health, raw scores, internal candidate lifecycle and a first observable loss-stage classification. `probe_samples.csv` and seven PNGs show representative full traces (offline), truth/fault intervals, samples, health, raw scores, candidate creation/cancellation/confirmation, uploads and report onsets. Compressed JSON includes all fields and intermediate pending states. Complete instrumented archives are regenerable under ignored `results/traces/v02-forensics`.

The observer delegates every analyzer call and returns it unchanged. Each instrumented run must be byte-equal to its original archived v0.1 result, including payload bytes and detections. A missing archive is an error, not silently regenerated evidence. Lifecycle labels are reconstructed from before/after pending/blocked states; raw calls and states are authoritative when creation and cancellation occur in one call. Loss-stage classifications are diagnostic priorities, not counterfactual proofs, and matching-window failures can have multiple contributors.

**Supported hypothesis:** bounded persistence from a frozen pre-candidate EMA, with the existing event threshold and three actual observations, may retain degraded evidence across health/score fluctuations and reduce episode churn. It may equally confirm drift and increase false reports. No old result demonstrates a net recall gain; development and validation must expose the tradeoff before new final seeds are opened.

**Unknown / outside evidence:** real faults, sensor calibration, spatial corroboration, concurrent spike/event timing, multiple faulty channels, actual RF energy and greenhouse performance. A stable offset versus a real sustained environmental step is also causally ambiguous to a single sensor; persistence cannot resolve it. The old primary same-channel recall gap must not be presented as proof that loosening FAULT gating fixes it.

Completed audit: **4,050** instrumented run results were byte-identical to their original archives; **2,700** event-method rows exported (900 physical labels x3 methods). Fresh legacy tests:67 passed; all18,900 old-test archive checksums and one fresh replay passed. Exact outputs are retained; no v0.1 metric or source change was needed.
